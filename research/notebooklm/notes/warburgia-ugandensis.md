# BOTANICAL DOSSIER: Warburgia ugandensis

## TAXONOMY & IDENTIFICATION
- Common Name: East African Greenheart / Pepper Bark Tree
- Scientific Binomial: Warburgia ugandensis Sprague
- Family: Canellaceae
- Indigenous East African Names: Muthiga (Kikuyu), Soket (Kipsigis), Apacha (Luo), Ol-sogonoi (Maasai), Musunui (Meru)
- Plant Part Examined: Leaves and sustainably harvested outer stem bark

## PHYTOCHEMICAL PROFILE
- Primary Active Constituents: Drimane sesquiterpenoids (warburganal, polygodial, muzigadial, ugandensidial), flavonol glycosides, tannins, essential sesquiterpene oils
- Standardization Markers: Polygodial / Warburganal concentration (minimum 0.8% dry weight in validated leaf harvests)

## EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE & CLINICAL DATA
- Evidence Level: GRADE_C
- Human Clinical Findings: Observational clinical use and ethnopharmacological trials in Kenya (KEMRI collaborative studies) report rapid symptomatic relief of acute upper respiratory congestion and pharyngeal irritation. Animal models demonstrate potent broad-spectrum antifungal (Candida albicans MIC < 12.5 ug/mL), antibacterial, and antimycobacterial activity.
- Mechanistic Action: Polygodial and warburganal act as non-specific membrane-disrupting sesquiterpenes that puncture fungal and bacterial lipid bilayers; simultaneously stimulate transient receptor potential ankyrin 1 (TRPA1) ion channels in mucosal membranes, clearing bronchial secretions.

## SAFETY, TOXICOLOGY & CONTRAINDICATIONS
- Safety Rating: Safe at micro-doses / culinary spice levels. Caution with high concentrated bark extracts.
- Known Contraindications: Contraindicated in pregnancy (mild oxytocic potential documented in ethno-veterinary medicine) and active gastric/peptic ulcers due to sharp, pungent peppery irritation.
- Drug-Herb Interactions: High-dose extracts may interact with liver CYP450 metabolism. Do not take concomitantly with strong pharmaceutical antacids.
- Daily Safe Dosage Range: 250mg - 500mg powdered leaf in warm water/tea blend; do not exceed 1.5g daily.

## TRADITIONAL ETHNOBOTANICAL USE
- Traditional Cultural Context: Revered as the ultimate "hospital tree" across Kenyan highland communities (Kikuyu, Maasai, Kipsigis). Bark and leaves are boiled with meat stews or steeped in hot water with honey for deep chest chills, malaria fever, and digestive heaviness.
- Traditional Claims: Cures all chest infections, stomach pain, fever, rheumatism, and internal parasites.

## CRITICAL DISTINCTION: TRADITION VS EVIDENCE
- Validated Traditional Uses: Respiratory tract clearing, pungent digestive stimulation, local oral/throat antimicrobial action.
- Unverified Folklore Claims: Direct malaria cure (in-vitro plasmodial inhibition does not equate to systemic clinical malaria clearance), cancer eradication.
- Safety Warnings for Marketing: Cannot market as antibiotic or malaria therapy. Formulate strictly as a pungent warming botanical tea / throat vapor balm for seasonal respiratory comfort.

## PROCESSING & QUALITY SPECIFICATIONS
- Optimal Drying / Extraction: Shift sourcing strictly from wild-tree trunk debarking (which kills wild trees) to **sustainable leaf pruning** from cultivated farm agroforestry plots. Low-temperature solar drying.
- Common Adulterants & Quality Risks: Adulteration with toxic lookalike barks from other highland species. Unsustainable wild-harvesting threatening endangered wild populations.
- Storage & Shelf-Life Stability: Protect from volatile oil evaporation. Sealed amber glass or double-wall foil barrier.
